Before we start: microphone permission

Why does True Access need your microphone? To describe what’s playing, the app has to hear it. When you tap “Yes, allow the microphone,” your phone will ask for permission. If your glasses are paired as a Bluetooth headset, pick their microphone next so the app hears the TV or video near you — not the room.

In this prototype, the sound is only used to check the volume level and figure out what’s playing. It is never recorded or saved.

Hands-free voice trigger

On iPhone, the always-on trigger only works when True Access is open in Safari — not in the installed app. This is an Apple limitation. The hold-to-talk button below works in Safari too.

Off.

When on, say "describe this" any time and the app describes what's playing — no tapping needed. The trigger only listens while "Start Listening" is active.

Wake-word setup (one time, free — enables the on-device engine)

The Porcupine wake-word engine hears "describe this" entirely on your phone — private, fast, and easy on the battery. Setup takes about 5 minutes:

  1. Make a free account at console.picovoice.ai (no credit card). Copy your AccessKey.
  2. In the console open Porcupine, type the wake phrase describe this, pick platform Web (WASM), train, and download the .ppn keyword file.
  3. Paste the key and choose the file below, then save.

Do not train a phrase starting with "Hey Meta" — the glasses wake their own assistant on "Hey Meta" before this app ever hears it. Plain "describe this" is the phrase to train.

Higher hears you more easily but may also fire on similar sounds. 0.65 is a good starting point.

Fallback: press and hold the button (or tap it once with a screen reader for a 6-second listen), say "describe this", release. Works in Safari on iPhone and Chrome on Android.

Battery note: the on-device wake word sips power and can stay on. Browser speech recognition keeps the mic and network radio busy — turn the trigger off when you're done watching.

About this prototype

This is an early working prototype of True Access, built by Mary Robinson. Microphone capture, voice output, and the full listening flow are real. Music identification is real too: the app fingerprints ten seconds of audio on your phone and matches it against the free AcoustID database (needs the free API key — steps are in FINGERPRINTING.md). TV shows and movies are not in any free database yet, so Demo mode with the sample programs stays available for testing the full flow. See ACCESSIBILITY.md in the project for the full accessibility audit and the VoiceOver test procedure.

Hands-free: set up "Hey Siri, describe this" with the free iPhone Shortcuts app (steps in SHORTCUTS.md) — no tapping needed. The in-app voice trigger ("describe this" / "audio describe") works while listening is on.

For Meta: True Access exposes deep links, a custom protocol (web+trueaccess://), URL actions, and a postMessage control API so it can be embedded or triggered from Meta's app. Integration points and the six asks are in META_INTEGRATION.md, with a machine-readable manifest at meta-integration.json.
